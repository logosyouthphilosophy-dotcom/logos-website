Eligibility

Who can enter

The competition is open to students anywhere in the world. You do not need to be based in the Middle East, and your school does not need to teach philosophy.

You are eligible if you will be in high school on 15 December 2026, which means anywhere from Grade 9 (Year 10) through Grade 12 (Year 13). If you graduate before that date, you are not eligible for this issue.

Submission Guidelines

Before you write

  1. Essays must reach us by 15 December 2026.
  2. Open to students worldwide who will be in high school on that date, from Grade 9 (Year 10) to Grade 12 (Year 13).
  3. Essays should be between 1,000 and 2,500 words, excluding your reference list.
  4. Write on any philosophical idea you like, from epistemology to metaphysics. We set no theme.
  5. Your essay should make an original, sustained contribution to a real debate in the field. We are looking for an argument you build and defend, not a summary of what other people have said.
  6. A panel of experts reviews every entry, Oxford PPE graduates among them. They judge the academic quality of the writing and the substance of the ideas.
  7. Write in English and submit your essay as a PDF.
  8. Cite in APA style, with a full list of references at the end of the paper.
  9. Use AI for early research only. Any essay containing AI-written text, or AI text run through a humaniser, is disqualified automatically.

On using AI

Use it to track down sources or check what an unfamiliar term means. That is research, and we have no problem with it.

Do not use it to write or reword your argument. Running AI text through a humaniser is still AI writing and we treat it the same way. Every entry gets checked, and disqualification is automatic.

The whole point of entering is to think something through yourself. An essay a machine could have produced tells our panel nothing about you.

The panel

How entries are judged

Quality of writing

Is the argument clear and properly evidenced? Can a reader follow exactly what you are claiming, and why you think it?

Quality of thought

Is the contribution original, and do you sustain it? We would rather read a serious attempt at a hard question than a tidy restatement of a familiar position.

Our panel of experts reads every entry, Oxford PPE graduates among them. The strongest essays go into the first issue of the Logos journal under your name.
