Become a member

One form, four fields. Membership is free, and it is how you get workshop invitations and access to the members portal.

What membership gets you

Registering makes you a member. It is free, and open to any secondary school student anywhere.

Workshops with professors

Free online workshops on how to read and write philosophy, open to registered students only. Professor Lizzie Fricker, who taught at Oxford for nearly thirty years, gave the first one.

The members portal

Workshop recordings and handouts, reading lists, and a noticeboard of announcements and deadlines.

Competition updates

Deadline reminders, guidance on what judges look for, and results before they go public. Submissions for the first issue close on 15 December 2026.

A direct line to us

Ask the team a question from inside the portal and we answer it. Use it to talk an essay idea through before you commit to it, or when the rules are unclear.

Four questions

Use an email you actually check. Your onboarding email and every workshop invitation go there.

We use your details to invite you to workshops, keep you posted on the competition, and match you to your portal account. Nothing is shared outside Logos. Already registered? Sign in at the members portal, or read the submission guidelines.

Then set up your portal account

Registering puts you on the register and gets you the emails. A portal account is what lets you sign in and read the recordings, handouts and noticeboard.

Use the same email for both and we match them for you.

Open the members portal
  1. Register above. You go onto the register straight away.
  2. Check your inbox. Your onboarding email explains what is coming and links you to the portal.
  3. Create your account. Same email address, choose a password, and you are in. About thirty seconds.
